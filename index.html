About me · The person behind the art

I go by Heart, and you can find me online as KHeart67. I’m an aspiring 2D and 3D animator and character designer who enjoys drawing, music, and visual storytelling. Portraits and original characters give me a way to explore feelings, personalities, and worlds that exist in my imagination.

I’m still building my skills through drawing projects, personal artwork, and hands-on practice. I’m drawn to goth and alternative self-expression, and the pink, purple, and black in this space reflect that side of me.

This website is both my creative portfolio and a record of my self-development. Alongside my artworks and characters, it shows what I’m learning about my strengths, the habits I’m working on, and the kind of person and artist I want to become.

My life mission & vision

My mission

To keep learning about myself while developing my drawing, animation, and character design skills through practice, experimentation, and feedback. I want to express myself honestly, treat people with kindness and fairness, and build habits that help me follow through on what matters to me.

In everyday life, this means setting manageable goals, making time to practice, and speaking up when I need help or have something to contribute.

My vision

To grow into a 2D and 3D animator and character designer who creates expressive characters and meaningful visual stories with other creative people. Beyond a career, I want to become more confident, consistent, and comfortable being myself.

I want a life where creativity and music have a place, where I can keep growing, and where I can support others without losing my own voice.

My strengths & weaknesses · Understanding myself

My strengths map connects patterns from my personality results, learning-style inventory, and character strengths. Looking at them together helps me understand why some approaches feel natural to me and why other parts of creative work need more support.

✧ Creative explorer

Openness, curiosity, and appreciation of beauty connect with my interest in art, music, and visual storytelling. They help me explore ideas and imagine characters, though I still need structure to turn those ideas into finished work.

♡ Compassionate collaborator

Kindness, fairness, honesty, and teamwork remind me to consider other people’s perspectives. These strengths matter when I listen, give feedback, or work with others. I’m also learning that being considerate includes speaking up about my own needs.

☾ Thoughtful visual learner

My learning-style results connect with how much images, demonstrations, and hands-on practice help me. Seeing a process and trying it myself gives me a clearer starting point. I can support this with visual plans and small practice exercises.

✿ Self-aware striver

I can participate and connect with people while also needing time to observe and work independently. Recognizing my difficulties with overthinking, confidence, and consistency helps me choose realistic ways to improve instead of expecting myself to be perfect.

Where I need support

I sometimes overthink my work, struggle to maintain motivation, or hesitate to speak up. These patterns can make starting, finishing, or sharing a project harder. They don’t erase my strengths, but they show me where I need a more supportive routine.

What the results helped me understand

Being creative and caring doesn’t automatically make me consistent or confident. My ideas need manageable deadlines, my learning needs examples and practice, and my confidence needs opportunities to ask questions and receive feedback. Understanding those connections gives me a clearer direction for growth.

Where I’m growing · My next steps

I’m working on overthinking, consistency, motivation, and speaking up. Small deadlines, visual plans, and finishable steps help me give my ideas a little more structure.

  • Consistency: choose one manageable drawing or animation exercise and make room for it in my week.
  • Overthinking: define a small goal for a piece, finish a draft, and choose one thing to improve next time.
  • Confidence: share a work in progress or ask for specific feedback instead of waiting until everything feels perfect.
  • Speaking up: practice asking questions, contributing ideas, and explaining what I need respectfully.

My goal is to support the creativity, care, and curiosity I already have with confidence and steady action. The growth map in my study corner gives these intentions a place to begin.

Personal and professional goals

My dream is to become an artist who can bring the things in my imagination to life. In the future, I hope to have my own studio, accept commissions, and create art for other people. One project that means a lot to me is the animated story I imagine for Rion and Khianna. It’s a big idea, and I know I still have a lot to learn, but I hope to tell their story someday. I also want to keep growing in music, hopefully with friends or a band.

Personally, I want to take more initiative, become more active and social, and have more confidence in myself. I want to learn how to begin tasks independently and work ahead of deadlines so I’m not always rushing or putting myself under unnecessary stress.

Personal growth plan

My first focus is color theory because I want to use it more confidently in illustrations and future models of my characters. Instead of expecting myself to learn everything at once, I want to follow clear lessons and apply one idea at a time to the digital artwork I’ve been trying to finish.

  1. Choose a small starting point. Follow a guided color exercise, such as testing a palette or studying values, before applying it to one part of my artwork.
  2. Work around my actual free time. At the beginning of each week, look for an available period and choose one manageable task. If my week is busy, make the task smaller instead of trying to rush everything.
  3. Use music to help me settle into working. Music sometimes helps when I feel unmotivated. I want to use that support while practicing starting before a deadline becomes urgent.
  4. Start school tasks earlier. Read the instructions when an assignment is given and decide what I can do first, even if I cannot finish much that day.
  5. Practice choosing my own next step. Clear instructions help me, but I want to gradually make small decisions independently before asking someone to guide me.
  6. Compare my work over time. Save dated versions and compare older pieces with newer ones, looking at color choices, expressions, details, and completion.

How I’ll review my progress

After completing a study or a piece, I can write a short note about what improved and what I still want to practice. At the end of a month when I have new work to compare, I can look back at the saved versions and decide on my next focus. If I haven’t had much time to draw, I can adjust the plan instead of treating it as a failure.

I haven’t intentionally tracked my art progress this way before, so this is a method I want to try—not an established habit yet.

Reflection on self-development

Through IR001, I started thinking more deeply about why I want to become an animator. It isn’t just a career that sounds interesting to me—it’s a dream connected to the characters and stories I want to bring to life. I also want other people to feel encouraged to pursue their own dreams. Building my portfolio made me think about my strengths and the habits that get in the way of using them. I feel confident with anatomy, facial expressions, and detailed drawing, but motivation, procrastination, and finishing work are still difficult for me. My friends sometimes remind me that my art turns out better when I actually finish it, and I think that’s something I need to work on. One of the hardest parts of making this website was figuring out where to start. Having clear instructions helped, but I also realized that I want to become more independent in deciding what to do next. I’m not completely sure how much I’ve changed yet, and I don’t want to pretend I already have everything figured out. For now, I have a clearer idea of what I want to practice: color theory, starting tasks earlier, and trusting myself more. I want to keep adding artworks to this portfolio so I can see my improvement little by little, even when it doesn’t feel obvious right away.

My website concept and future updates

I chose pink and purple because they’re my favorite colors, and I like how the playful fonts match them. My characters show the alternative style I’m drawn to. Sir Phil described my art style as “alt/gothic,” and that’s a description I connect with. I want visitors to recognize that personality in my work and understand the kind of art I enjoy creating.

After this course, I want to keep using this website to upload finished artworks, share my characters, and show my progress. It can become a place where I compare my older and newer pieces and see how my style develops over time.

I can update it when I finish a piece, add short notes about what I learned, and revisit my goals as my interests and skills grow. The portfolio doesn’t have to stay exactly the same—it can grow with me.
